Classical Breakdown

John Banther takes classical music fans behind the scenes with interviews, deep dives, and analysis. Episodes released bi-weekly on Tuesdays. Produced by WETA Classical in Washington, D.C.

Image

Latest Episode

Alexander Scriabin: music, philosophy, and the end of the world!

November 10, 2026

Was this a misunderstood composer or just another man with a tower sized ego? John and Evan explore his life and music, controversies, how his music evolved, the philosophy that shaped his music, his sudden end, and give you several recommended works to listen to.

    We learned all about Mozart's final work last week with Steven Fox, and now we get to put our ears to work and enjoy a full performance.

  • Your Guide to Mozart's Requiem with Steven Fox

    October 27, 2026

    Conductor and Mozart Requiem lover Steven Fox tells us everything we need to know about this iconic work. From how it was completed, how the text fits within the music, the drama of Mozart's death, to what to listen for!
    • Show Notes

  • Snoopy and the Gang come to life in Ellen Taaffe Zwilich's Peanuts Gallery!

    October 13, 2026

    What kind of dance is Snoopy's favorite? What is Schroeder's favorite Beethoven sonata? And will Charlie ever kick the football? John Banther and Evan Keely learn all about that and more in the imaginative piano concerto, Peanuts Gallery, by American Composer Ellen Zwilich.
    • Show Notes

  • William Schuman: The composer that shaped Juilliard, the Lincoln Center, and American music

    September 29, 2026

    Why is it that this composer's name is probably not known to you? John Banther and Evan Keely explore some of his extraordinary music and achievements in the arts, including how he shaped American art institutions and his very unusual start in composing music.
    • Show Notes

  • What is a Harp with Nadia Pessoa: Ancient origins to modern innovations!

    September 15, 2026

    Not too many instruments in the orchestra have a history that spans the centuries, and use of the harp! Nadia Pessoa, a Washington, DC-area harpist, shares with us the harp's use in the orchestra from around 1600 to now, answers John's burning questions, and plays for us, including a recent viral tune on the harp.
    • Show Notes

  • Bonus! A performance of Handel's Water Music

    September 8, 2026

    Enjoy a Concertgebouw Chamber Orchestra recording of the music we learned about last week, plus a listener comment that helps clarify the situation after Queen Anne's death!
    • Show Notes

  • Handel's Water Music; a King's bid to lift his reputation and one smelly river

    September 1, 2026

    Why did Handel write this, and what would it have been like on the river's edge? John Banther and Linda Carducci explore how Handel went above and beyond for a King and even what it would have been like for the musicians.
    • Show Notes

  • Season 7 Recommended Summer Listening!

    June 23, 2026

    It's time again to look at some recently recorded albums for us to enjoy and grow with this summer. John Banther has 4 albums to recommend, and one of them includes a remarkable composer's story!
    • Show Notes

  • Tosca! Puccini's tragic opera on love, lust, and brutality

    June 9, 2026

    Why watch a thriller on a Friday night when you can watch the dramatic and compact "blood and guts" opera by Puccini that is... Tosca! John Banther and Linda Carducci give you the historical and cultural context to the plot, show you specific themes hidden throughout, and highlight 4 show-stopping moments.
    • Show Notes

  • The Montgomery Variations: A Piece of American History by Margaret Bonds

    May 26, 2026

    Music can be just as much of a historical document as letters or legislation, and we see that directly in this episode with music by Bonds written in 1963. John Banther and Evan Keely show you what to listen for, how Bonds adapts a well-known melody, the historical context, and more.
    • Show Notes

  • Haydn's Trumpet Concerto: Revolutionary in ways you didn't know!

    May 12, 2026

    There is so much more to this humble concerto than you realize, because the context of hearing a trumpet do something unheard of before has been lost. We show you what made this concerto possible, how Haydn was revolutionary, and why each recording is a little bit different.
    • Show Notes

  • Leoš Janáček: An exploration of his life, cultural identity, and unique sound

    April 28, 2026

    Another listener request! John Banther and Bill Bukowski explore the life of Leos Janacek and look at 2 specific subjects to understand this composer's original sound: Speech Melodies and Modality.
